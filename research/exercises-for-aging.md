# PT-10: military-style training for healthy aging

**Scope.** Practical redesign guidance for generally independent adults in their 40s–70s. Evidence grades: **Strong** = guideline or consistent systematic-review evidence; **Moderate** = smaller review/RCT plus sound transfer to this app; **Weak** = military doctrine, biomechanics, or expert inference without direct aging trials.

## 1. Top findings

- Keep the military feel, but train **movement qualities**, not punishment: squat/stand, hinge, push, pull, carry/grip, brace, move laterally, get off the floor, balance, and produce force quickly. Current Army doctrine includes preparation, hip-stability, core, and recovery drills; Marine programming likewise separates movement prep, strength/power, speed/agility/endurance, and flexibility/mobility.[1][3]
- Adults 65+ should combine aerobic, strength and balance work; U.S. guidance calls for strength work at least twice weekly, and WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly. Ten minutes daily is a useful minimum dose, not the whole program; add brisk walking or equivalent.[5][6]
- Balance and functional exercise reduce falls. A Cochrane review of 108 trials found exercise reduced fall rate, with especially useful evidence for standing balance and functional tasks.[7]
- Preserve **power**, not just slow endurance. High-velocity resistance training—moving the up phase fast while staying controlled—improves several mobility tests in older adults, and a small bodyweight RCT improved chair-rise power and Timed Up-and-Go.[9][10]
- The Army’s five-event AFT emphasizes deadlift/hinge strength, pushing, sprint-drag-carry capacity, bracing and aerobic endurance. PT-10 cannot reproduce loaded events, but it can train their underlying patterns.[2]
- Bone benefits require meaningful, progressive loading. LIFTMOR supports supervised heavy resistance/impact training in screened women with low bone mass; it does **not** prove that unsupervised jumping is right for every older adult.[11]

## 2. Recommended exercise library

### Preparation and mobility

1. **Power march + arm swing** — *power, balance, aerobic.* Builds foot clearance and reciprocal gait. Tall posture; drive opposite arm/knee. **Easier:** slow march holding chair. **Harder:** faster march or brief high knees. **Evidence: Strong**—multicomponent activity and balance guidance.[6][7]
2. **Bend-and-reach hip hinge** — *mobility, posterior-chain pattern.* Rehearses lifting without repeated spinal rounding. Soften knees, push hips back, long spine. **Easier:** hands to thighs. **Harder:** reach forward or hold light backpack. **Moderate**—Army Preparation Drill plus AFT hinge demand.[1][2]
3. **Supported rear lunge + reach** — *strength, balance, hip mobility.* Trains stepping reactions and single-leg control. Step back softly; front knee tracks over toes. **Easier:** shallow range/chair support. **Harder:** full reverse lunge. **Strong**—functional/balance evidence; Army drill use.[1][7]
4. **Standing windmill** — *thoracic/hip mobility, balance.* Encourages controlled rotation while upright. Hinge slightly; rotate through upper back, not a forced toe touch. **Easier:** hands to opposite thigh. **Harder:** longer reach. **Moderate**—Army preparation exercise; direct aging evidence limited.[1]
5. **Heel-toe raises** — *ankle strength, balance.* Supports gait, push-off and toe clearance. Rise tall, then lift toes without rocking hips. **Easier:** two hands on chair. **Harder:** single-leg calf raise. **Strong**—balance/functional programs reduce falls.[7]
6. **Wall slide / overhead reach** — *shoulder mobility, posture.* Counters forward posture and prepares pushing. Ribs down; slide only through painless range. **Easier:** alternating reaches. **Harder:** lift hands slightly from wall. **Moderate**—military movement-prep principle; exercise-specific aging evidence limited.[3]

### Strength and power

7. **Chair squat / sit-to-stand** — *leg strength, function.* Directly trains a key independence task. Feet under knees; lean slightly; stand through whole foot. **Easier:** higher chair/use hands. **Harder:** lower chair or light backpack. **Strong**—systematic review supports task-specific performance.[12]
8. **Fast chair rise** — *power.* Stand “fast but smooth,” sit down slowly; power is important for stairs and recovering balance. **Easier:** hands on thighs. **Harder:** no hands/light load. **Strong**—power meta-analysis; bodyweight RCT.[9][10]
9. **Split squat** — *leg/hip strength, balance.* Builds unilateral capacity without a moving step. Drop straight down; front foot stays planted. **Easier:** short range/chair support. **Harder:** full range or rear-foot kickstand. **Strong**—resistance and multicomponent training improve strength/function.[8][17]
10. **Low step-up** — *strength, power, balance.* Closely matches stairs and obstacle negotiation. Whole foot on step; control down. **Easier:** bottom stair with rail. **Harder:** higher step or fast controlled ascent. **Strong**—functional and balance training evidence.[7][9]
11. **Bodyweight good morning** — *hinge, glutes/hamstrings, back endurance.* Fills the posterior-chain gap. Hands on ribs; hips back; stop before spine rounds. **Easier:** wall tap hinge. **Harder:** backpack Romanian deadlift. **Moderate**—AFT hinge relevance and resistance guidance.[2][8]
12. **Glute bridge** — *hip strength, core.* Useful low-impact posterior-chain work. Brace, squeeze glutes, avoid over-arching. **Easier:** shorter lift. **Harder:** marching or single-leg bridge. **Moderate**—consistent resistance principle; limited exercise-specific aging trials.[8][17]
13. **Wall/incline push-up** — *upper-body strength, core.* Scalable military pushing with less wrist/shoulder load. Body moves as one unit; elbows about 30–45°. **Easier:** wall. **Harder:** counter, bench, then floor. **Strong**—progressive resistance guidance; military pushing pattern.[2][8]
14. **Hand-release push-up** — *push strength/endurance, upper-back control.* Mirrors AFT pattern. From floor or incline, pause/release lightly, re-brace, press. **Easier:** high incline. **Harder:** floor. **Moderate**—military specificity; aging benefit inferred from resistance evidence.[2][8]
15. **Band row (optional band)** — *pull strength, posture, grip.* Balances frequent pushing; pulling is otherwise hard to train safely without equipment. Anchor band securely; pull elbows toward ribs. **Easier:** light band. **Harder:** stronger band/pause. **Strong**—major muscle groups need progressive resistance.[6][8]
16. **Towel isometric row** — *pull, grip, core.* No-anchor substitute: loop towel around feet while seated, pull against it without jerking. **Easier:** gentle 5-second pulls. **Harder:** stronger 10-second pulls. **Weak**—reasonable pattern substitute, but direct outcome evidence is sparse.[8]
17. **Calf raise** — *lower-leg strength, balance.* Helps walking and stair push-off. Rise straight up; lower slowly. **Easier:** bilateral with support. **Harder:** single-leg or faster up/slow down. **Strong**—resistance plus balance evidence.[7][8]
18. **Step jack** — *aerobic, coordination, low-impact power.* Keeps military cadence without mandatory jumping. Step wide as arms rise; land softly. **Easier:** arms below shoulder height. **Harder:** jumping jack if joints, pelvic floor and balance tolerate it. **Moderate**—multicomponent guidance; specific move not trial-proven.[6]
19. **Lateral step/shuffle** — *agility, hip strength, balance.* Trains a plane often missed by squats and walking. Stay low; toes forward; do not cross feet initially. **Easier:** side steps. **Harder:** quicker shuffle. **Strong**—standing balance/functional training.[7]
20. **Small squat jump or rapid calf raise** — *power, bone loading.* Use only after a pain-free squat and stable landing. Quiet landing, knees track over toes. **Easier:** fast calf raise. **Harder:** low jump. **Moderate**—power evidence and supervised bone-loading RCT; transfer requires screening.[9][11]

### Core, balance, grip and ground skill

21. **Dead bug / heel tap** — *core control.* Trains bracing without repeated loaded spinal flexion. Keep ribs down and low back comfortable. **Easier:** one heel tap. **Harder:** opposite arm/leg extension. **Moderate**—biomechanical rationale; direct aging trials limited.[14]
22. **Bird dog** — *core, back/hip endurance, balance.* Reach long, not high; keep pelvis level. **Easier:** one limb. **Harder:** opposite arm/leg with pause. **Moderate**—stability rationale plus multicomponent training evidence.[7][14]
23. **Incline/front plank** — *core endurance, shoulder stability.* Brace and breathe; stop before sagging. **Easier:** wall/counter. **Harder:** floor or shoulder taps. **Strong**—AFT bracing pattern and scalable resistance.[2][8]
24. **Side plank** — *lateral core, hip stability.* Builds trunk and hip control. **Easier:** wall or knees bent. **Harder:** feet stacked. **Moderate**—Army core/hip-stability doctrine; direct aging evidence limited.[1]
25. **Single-leg stand with reach** — *balance, foot/hip control.* Stand near support; reach lightly in several directions. **Easier:** kickstand stance. **Harder:** less hand support or head turns. **Strong**—balance training reduces falls.[7]
26. **Half-kneel/tactical stand-up** — *ground transfer, mobility, whole-body strength.* Practice rising with a chair nearby; use a stable sequence rather than speed. **Easier:** one hand on chair. **Harder:** no hands or controlled stand-to-floor. **Strong**—functional-task and fall-prevention evidence.[7][12]
27. **Suitcase carry/march (optional backpack or jug)** — *grip, core, gait.* Carry on one side without leaning; switch halfway. **Easier:** light object/march in place. **Harder:** heavier load. **Moderate**—military carry demand and resistance rationale.[2][8]
28. **Towel crush/wring** — *grip.* Short hard squeezes add a missing capacity. Wrists neutral; no painful twisting. **Easier:** soft towel. **Harder:** thicker towel or timed hold. **Moderate**—grip strength is a strong health marker, though evidence that isolated grip work changes health outcomes is indirect.[13]

## 3. Proposed 10-minute format and rotation

**Every session = exactly 10:00:** 1:00 dynamic preparation (four 15-second moves), **8 × 1:00 stations**, 1:00 recovery. Retain levels: Recruit 30s work/30s reset, Soldier 40/20, Ranger 50/10. “Ranger” should mean excellent form and appropriate progression—not compulsory impact.

- **Day A — Strength/base:** chair squat, incline push-up, hinge, band/towel row, split squat, bridge, suitcase march, front plank.
- **Day B — Power/balance:** power march, fast chair rise, step-up, step jack, lateral step, rapid calf raise or small jump, single-leg reach, half-kneel stand-up.
- **Day C — Resilience/mobility:** rear lunge, wall slide, calf raise, bird dog, side plank, hip-stability leg raise, dead bug, easy bear hover or incline mountain climber.
- **Week:** Mon A, Tue B, Wed C, Thu A, Fri B, Sat C, Sun recovery-biased C or easy walk/mobility. Progress one variable at a time: range, support, speed, then load. An optional $10–20 resistance band and a sturdy chair materially improve pulling, scaling and balance safety.

## 4. Drop or modify from the current app

- **Replace routine sit-ups, V-ups and flutter kicks** with dead bug, bird dog and side plank. Repeated hip-flexor-dominant trunk flexion is not uniquely necessary; small biomechanics studies favor selected curl-up/stability options for a better abdominal challenge-to-spinal-load tradeoff.[14]
- **Regress burpees and 8-count body builders** to a hands-on-chair step-back burpee or tactical stand-up. Keep full versions optional for trained, pain-free users; speed, floor transition and impact compound fatigue and fall/joint demands.
- **Default jumping jacks/high knees to step jacks/power march.** Offer jumping only as a progression, not an age test.
- **Replace diamond push-ups** with incline or hand-release push-ups; diamonds add wrist/elbow/shoulder demand without filling a missing movement category.
- **Change forward lunges to supported reverse lunges or split squats** for easier balance and depth control.
- **De-emphasize wall sits.** They are acceptable as a short isometric option, but chair rises and squats train usable range and power.
- **Scale mountain climbers and bear crawls** to an incline or bear hover when wrists, shoulders or trunk control are limiting.
- **Add what is missing:** pulling, grip/carry, calf/ankle work, lateral movement, explicit balance, hip abductor work, hinge strength, controlled power and ground-transfer practice.

## 5. Safety notes

- Age alone is not a contraindication. Start with pain-free range, stable support and an effort around **5–7/10**; finish each interval with form intact. Muscle effort is expected; sharp pain, chest pressure, faintness, unusual breathlessness, new neurologic symptoms or joint instability means stop.
- New exercisers, people with recent falls/surgery, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, significant arthritis, neuropathy, vertigo, or known osteoporosis/vertebral fracture should get individualized medical or physical-therapy guidance before impact, deep floor transitions or hard intervals.
- For osteoporosis or vertebral-fracture risk, emphasize upright strength, balance, hip hinge and spinal extension; avoid forceful/end-range loaded spinal flexion. Impact must be individualized.[15]
- Put balance drills beside a counter, never beside loose furniture. Use a non-slip floor and secure any band anchor. Do not hold breath during hard efforts.
- The app could periodically suggest simple function checks—not competitive scores—such as five chair stands or the CDC Timed Up-and-Go; concerning decline or unsteadiness warrants clinical assessment.[16]
